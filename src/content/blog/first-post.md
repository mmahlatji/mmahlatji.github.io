Minimalism gets a bad reputation. Strip too much away and you're left with something sterile — functional, sure, but lifeless. The websites I keep coming back to are the ones that feel *considered*: a weight to the type, a beat in the timing, a single bright detail that earns its place.

That's the tension I try to hold in my own work. Restraint on the surface, intention underneath.

## Less, but better

The goal isn't to remove things. It's to remove everything that isn't doing work.

> Simplicity is not the absence of clutter — it's the presence of order.

When I design a page, I ask three questions before adding anything:

1. **Does it help someone understand faster?**
2. **Does it reward attention?**
3. **Could it be quieter?**

If the answer to all three is no, it doesn't ship.

## Motion as meaning

Animation isn't decoration. Used well, it explains *where things came from* and *where they're going* — spatial relationships you'd otherwise have to describe in words.

A card that lifts on hover tells you it's clickable. A section that fades in as you scroll gives the page rhythm. None of it needs to shout.

## The bright detail

A monochrome palette doesn't mean monotone. When almost everything is a shade of gray, the few things that are *white* — a headline, a dot, a hover state — become the whole personality of the page.

This site is my attempt at that. A near-black canvas, a single accent that's just pure brightness, and just enough motion to feel alive.
